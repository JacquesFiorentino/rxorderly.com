Chapter 1

What RxOrderly Does for You

First and foremost, RxOrderly is going to make the day-to-day business of managing your prescriptions practically effortless.

No more juggling pill bottles to figure out which one is running low. No more frantic phone calls to the pharmacy on a Sunday evening. No more standing at the counter trying to remember the name of the medication, the strength, and the doctor who prescribed it. No more lost paper schedules taped to the refrigerator.

RxOrderly takes care of all of that for you. The app keeps a careful, up-to-date record of every prescription you take, knows exactly how much you have on hand, knows exactly when you’ll need a refill, and quietly speaks up when it’s time to ask for one. When that moment comes, it composes the refill request for you, ready to go in a single tap.

That alone would be a wonderful little tool to have.

But here is the joyful part: that’s just where the app begins.

Far Beyond Refills

RxOrderly is also going to help you with:

  • Your daily medication routines. Set up the times you take each medication, and the app will gently remind you when each one is due — every single dose, every single day, automatically.

  • Your therapies and care activities. Physical therapy exercises, blood sugar checks, eye drops, breathing treatments, anything that isn’t a pill — RxOrderly schedules and tracks it just like a medication.

  • Coming home from the hospital. A hospital discharge often comes with a stack of new prescriptions, a brand-new schedule, and a head still tired from being unwell. RxOrderly is the perfect place to land. Type in the new medications, set up the new schedule, and a chaotic regimen becomes orderly and accessible — usually within an hour of getting home.

  • Preparing for a doctor’s visit. The Doctor Visit Prep wizard walks you through seven friendly questions and produces a tidy, printable sheet you can hand your doctor — your medication list, the symptoms you’ve noticed, the questions you want to ask, the changes you’d like to discuss. No more “I had a question, but I can’t remember what it was.”

  • Your Medical ID card. A wallet-sized card with your medications, allergies, blood type, emergency contacts, and a special note for first responders. Print it, carry it, update it any time. If you ever need it, you’ll be very glad it’s there.

  • Researching your medications. Look up any drug — interactions, side effects, warnings — straight from official FDA and U.S. National Library of Medicine sources.

  • Finding ways to save. RxOrderly can tell you which prescription assistance programs you might qualify for, and points you to Medicare resources, NeedyMeds, RxAssist, and a free counselor in your area.

  • Keeping a complete history. Every refill, every dose, every visit, every adjustment — recorded and easy to look up later.

In short, RxOrderly is your daily partner. Not a replacement for your doctor. Not a replacement for your pharmacist. A partner — one that quietly remembers everything so you don’t have to.

Most Important Idea In This Book. The more RxOrderly knows about you, the more it does for you. Please don’t skip this part. It is the difference between a useful app and an extraordinary one. The app is a partner, and like any good partner, it can only help you with what you’ve told it. The more complete the information — your medications, your doses, your prescribers, your pharmacies, your insurance cards, your emergency contacts, your allergies, your blood type — the more the app can quietly do on your behalf. That up-front investment is what unlocks everything in this book. With your information filled in: Refill requests compose themselves, ready to send. Daily routines line up in a few taps. A Doctor Visit Prep sheet is ready in under a minute when you need one. Your Medical ID card is ready to print before anyone asks for it. Refill reminders fire on their own, days before you would have noticed. It is well worth taking the time — perhaps a quiet hour with your medication bottles and your insurance cards spread out in front of you — to fill everything in completely. And you don’t have to do it all in one sitting. Start and stop as many times as you’d like. Work at your own pace. The important thing is to do it completely. That time pays you back every single day for as long as you use the app. So please — take the time.  It is the kindest thing you can do for yourself with RxOrderly.

Your Privacy

Your data is strictly private and encrypted. Nobody has access to it except you. Period. Full stop.

Your medication names, your doctors, your pharmacies, your personal details — every bit of it lives on your phone and your phone alone. RxOrderly cannot see it. Rivka SVCS LLC, the company that makes the app, cannot see it. There are no servers holding your information, no back-door access, no way at all.

When you ask the app to send a refill request, your phone composes the message and you send it from your own email or text app — even then, nothing passes through us. You’re always the one in the driver’s seat.

When you back up your data to a file (Chapter 21), the app can encrypt the backup with a password only you know. We’ll walk you through exactly how when we get there.

This is foundational. It is how the app was built from the very first line of code, and it will never change.

Your Medications List

Your medications list is the beating heart of RxOrderly. Everything — supply tracking, refill requests, reminders, your Medical ID, Doctor Visit Prep — flows from the medications you’ve entered. So this chapter is one of the most important.

Good news: it’s also genuinely easy.

Getting to Your List

From the Dashboard, tap Show More if needed, then tap the tile labeled Medications &** Care Activities, Prescribers ****&**** Retailers. You’ll see a small menu with four choices: Medications, Care Activities, Prescribers, and Pharmacies / Retailers. Tap Medications**.

If you haven’t added any medications yet, you’ll see a friendly message: “No medications added yet. Tap the button above to add your first medication.”

Adding a Medication

Tap the big blue button at the top: + Add Medication or Supplement. The Add Medication form opens, and we’ll walk through it field by field.

Figure 4-2 [Screenshot to come]
The top half of the Add Medication form.

The Name

Start typing the medication’s name. After three letters, suggestions appear from a built-in list of more than 28,000 medications — the same one the U.S. National Library of Medicine uses. Tap a suggestion and the name fills in with the proper spelling and capitalization.

Why This Matters. The official spelling is sometimes a little unusual — for example, you’ll see medications spelled with mixed capitals like “hydroCHLOROthiazide.” That’s called tall-man lettering, a safety convention pharmacists use to keep similarly-named drugs from getting confused. Letting the app fill in the canonical name guarantees you and your pharmacy are talking about exactly the same thing.

If your medication isn’t in the list (a brand-new prescription, perhaps), just type the full name yourself. RxOrderly will accept it.

Dose and Unit

Look at your prescription bottle. Most labels say something like “Lisinopril 10 mg tablet”.

  • Dose is the number: 10.

  • Unit is what comes after: mg. Tap the Unit field and pick from a list. For pills, mg (milligrams) is by far the most common. For liquids you’ll see mL. For very small doses, mcg (micrograms). For insulin and a few others, IU. There’s also tabs, capsules, drops, and so on.

How Often (Frequency)

Figure 4-3 [Screenshot to come]
The Frequency picker open, listing options like Daily, Twice daily, etc.

Tap the Frequency field and pick from the list: Daily, Twice daily, Three times daily, Four times daily, Weekly, Monthly, As Needed, and a handful of others. Pick what matches your prescription instructions.

This field alone is enough to make refill timing work: if you tell RxOrderly you take a pill twice daily, the app knows a 60-pill bottle lasts 30 days. (Setting up the exact times of day is a separate step, covered in Chapter 10.)

Prescriber and Pharmacy

Figure 4-4 [Screenshot to come]
The lower half of the Add Medication form — prescriber, pharmacy, category, icon.

Tap Prescriber to pick the doctor who prescribed this medication. If you haven’t added that doctor yet, you can pop over to Chapter 5 first, or simply skip this field for now and come back to it later. Same with Pharmacy / Retailer — pick from your list, or come back after Chapter 6.

These fields are optional, but they’re what makes refill requests compose themselves automatically. Worth filling in when you can.

Category

A small label that helps RxOrderly organize your meds. The choices are Prescription, Over-the-Counter, Supplement, Vitamin, and a few others. New medications default to Prescription. Adjust if needed.

An Icon (Optional but Fun)

Figure 4-5 [Screenshot to come]
The icon selection sheet.

Tap Select Icon and choose something that looks like your medication — a round white tablet, a blue capsule, an inhaler, a liquid bottle, a patch, an injection pen. Totally optional, but it makes the medication easy to spot at a glance later. (Don’t worry about getting it perfect.)

Saving

When the form looks good, tap Save. The medication appears in your list. That’s it — one down.

Now repeat for each medication you take. Most people are done in fifteen or twenty minutes, even with a long list.

Pace Yourself. You don’t have to enter every medication in one sitting. Stop whenever you’d like and come back later. Your list saves automatically. Just remember to come back and finish — the app gets dramatically more useful once your full list is in.

Editing a Medication

Figure 4-6 [Screenshot to come]
Edit Medication page on an existing entry.

Tap any medication in your list to view its details, then tap the pencil icon to edit. Change anything that needs updating — a new dose, a different unit, a new prescriber — and tap Save.

Changing Prescriber or Pharmacy

Figure 4-7 [Screenshot to come]
The “Change Pharmacy” confirmation.

If your doctor changes, or you switch pharmacies, tap Change Prescriber or Change Pharmacy on the medication’s page. RxOrderly will confirm before making the change, then update the link.

Deactivating, Not Deleting

If you stop taking a medication, please don’t delete it. Instead, edit the medication and switch off the Active toggle.

Here’s why: your history of doses, refills, and adjustments stays intact — useful for your records, your doctor, and your own peace of mind. The inactive medication simply drops off the daily lists.

If you start taking it again later, just switch Active back on.

Chapter 5

Your Prescribers

Doctors, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, specialists, clinics — in RxOrderly we call them all prescribers, since any one of them might write a prescription.

Why Prescribers Matter

Three good reasons to take a few minutes to add yours:

  • Refill requests need to know who prescribed each medication. The right doctor’s contact details are dropped into the message for you, automatically.

  • Doctor Visit Prep sheets (Chapter 15) need to know whom you’re seeing — the sheet is addressed to a specific doctor.

  • Your Medical ID card (Chapter 16) lists your prescribers, so paramedics and emergency room staff know who to call.

From the Dashboard, tap Show More, then the combined tile, then Prescribers.

Address, Phone, Email

Enter whatever you have on hand from your appointment card or the practice’s website. Phone numbers format themselves nicely as you type. The email field is what RxOrderly uses to address a refill request to this prescriber, if your doctor’s office accepts refill requests by email.

Primary Prescriber

Switch on Primary Prescriber for your main doctor — the one who manages most of your care. They’ll appear first in lists and be suggested as the default contact on refill requests. You can only have one Primary Prescriber at a time; choosing a new one quietly moves the flag from the old one.

Refill Contact

Switch on Refill Contact if this prescriber’s office is somewhere you’d send a refill request. Most of your doctors will be Refill Contacts. Switch it off for, say, a specialist who only sees you for consultations and doesn’t manage prescriptions — that way they don’t clutter up your refill picker.

Notes

The Notes field is for anything else you want to remember: office hours, your favorite nurse’s name, a parking tip, the receptionist’s direct extension. RxOrderly doesn’t do anything special with this text — it’s just there for you.

Saving

Tap Save. Repeat for each of your prescribers. Even seniors with long histories usually have just a handful, so this part goes quickly.

Inactive Prescribers

Same idea as medications: when a doctor retires or you stop seeing them, switch off the Active toggle. They’ll drop off the daily lists but stay in your history. Turn them back on anytime if you go back.

Chapter 6

Your Pharmacies ****&**** Retailers

Pharmacies, mail-order suppliers, the vitamin shop on the corner — in RxOrderly we call them all retailers, since any one of them might be where you go to refill or restock.

Why Retailers Matter

Just like prescribers, the more RxOrderly knows about the places that supply your medications, the more it can do for you:

  • Refill requests go to a specific pharmacy — the right phone number, the right email, the right address. The app fills all that in for you.

  • You may use more than one pharmacy — a local one for everyday medications, a mail-order one for the maintenance prescriptions. RxOrderly lets you assign each medication to the right pharmacy and routes refills accordingly.

  • Your Medical ID card (Chapter 16) can list your pharmacy so first responders and emergency room staff know where your prescription history lives.

Getting to Your Retailers List

From the Dashboard, tap Show More, then the combined tile, then Pharmacies (sometimes labeled Retailers).

Phone Number

Helpful Tip. If your pharmacy has a separate prescription refill line (many do — it’s often a different number from the main store), use that one. When you request a refill by phone, RxOrderly will offer this number to call.

Notes

Anything else you want to remember — opening hours, your pharmacist’s name, parking, the secret good entrance around the back.

Saving

Tap Save. Repeat for any other pharmacies you use.

More Than One Pharmacy? Welcome to the Club.

Many seniors use a mix: a local pharmacy for occasional and short-term prescriptions, plus a mail-order pharmacy for the maintenance ones. Add them all here. Chapter 7

Supply on Hand

Welcome to the most-used screen in the entire app. Supply on Hand answers the question we all want a quiet, honest answer to: am I going to run out of anything soon?

Now that your medications, doctors, and pharmacies are in, this screen is genuinely useful.

Getting There

From the Dashboard, tap the very first tile: Supply on Hand. (It’s the one with the pill bottle icon.)

Figure 7-1 [Screenshot to come]
The Supply on Hand list showing several medications with their days left and units on hand.

Reading a Row

Each medication gets one neat row. From left to right, every row shows:

Figure 7-2 [Screenshot to come]
A single Supply on Hand row, close up.

  • The medication name — with its icon if you picked one.

  • Days Left — how many days until you run out, based on the dose and frequency you entered. The number turns amber when it’s under two weeks, red when it’s under a week. A peaceful gray when you’ve got plenty.

  • On Hand — how many units (pills, mL of liquid, etc.) you currently have. The app keeps this number current as you log refills and routine doses.

  • Last Unit — the date your supply is projected to run out, in plain English (“Aug 14”).

Helpful Tip. If a row has a small clock icon beside it, that means RxOrderly has already noticed it’s getting low and queued up a refill reminder for you. The app is looking out for you.

Tapping a Row for History

Figure 7-3 [Screenshot to come]
The History sheet that appears when you tap a Supply row.

Chapter 21

Backups: Your Safety Net

Everything RxOrderly knows about you lives on your phone — which is wonderful for privacy (Chapter 22) but does mean that if you lose your phone, you’d lose the data along with it. The fix is simple: make backups, store them somewhere safe.

What we recommend. Take a backup often — at least whenever you add a medication, change a dose, or update your refill schedule. Then keep a copy of the backup file (or files) on a separate device — another phone, a tablet, a computer, or a cloud drive like iCloud or Google Drive. That way, if your phone is lost, stolen, broken, or replaced with a new one, your medication history is safe and you can restore it onto the new device.

Getting There

Open Settings (the gear in the top-right of the Dashboard), and scroll down to the Data &** Backup** section.

Figure 21-1 [Screenshot to come]

Creating a Backup

Figure 21-2 [Screenshot to come]
The Create Backup dialog with the optional password prompt and hint field.

Tap Back Up Now (or Create Backup). RxOrderly offers an optional password:

  • Leave the password blank and the backup file is unencrypted — convenient, but anyone who gets the file can read it.

  • Enter a password and RxOrderly encrypts the file with AES-256, the same encryption used by banks and governments. The file is unreadable without the password — even to us. There’s a small optional hint field too, in case you want a memory jog later.

The app saves the backup file to your phone’s downloads folder and offers a share sheet so you can send the file to wherever it’s safest for you — email it to yourself, save it to iCloud or Google Drive, attach it to a message to a trusted family member.

Pace Yourself. Use a password you can actually remember. If you forget the password, the file becomes unrecoverable — even Rivka SVCS can’t open it. That’s the price of real privacy: it’s real for you too.

Tap Restore Data from Backup File, pick the backup file (from downloads, email, Drive, wherever), enter the password if you used one, and confirm. RxOrderly replaces the current data with what was in the file.

This feature isn’t enabled in the current version of the app; when it ships, it will be turned off by default, and you’ll be able to turn it on from this section.

Chapter 22 explains, in plain English, why your data stays on your phone in the first place — RxOrderly’s privacy promise.

Chapter 22

Your Privacy in Plain English

You shared a brief version of this in Chapter 1. Here’s the longer version, in plain language, written so that you can read it once and know exactly where you stand.

The Short Version

Your data is strictly private. Nobody has access to it except you. Period. Full stop.

The Six Promises

Six commitments are built into RxOrderly — not just policy decisions that could be reversed, but architectural choices that shape the way the whole app works.

1. We Will Never Sell Your Data

Not now, not in the future, not under any circumstance. There is no data-monetization business model here. RxOrderly is funded by Premium subscriptions, and that’s it.

2. Your Information Stays on Your Phone

Your medication list, your doctors, your pharmacies, your insurance cards, your personal details — every bit of it is stored only in the app’s database on your phone. RxOrderly has no servers holding copies of your information, and the company that makes the app cannot see it.

This is an architectural decision, not a policy promise. The app simply doesn’t send your data anywhere. The infrastructure to do so doesn’t exist.

3. When You Send a Refill Request, You Send It — Not Us

The composed message from a refill request opens in your own phone’s email, text, or phone app. You tap Send. Nothing passes through RxOrderly’s systems. The pharmacy receives the message from you directly, with your phone’s normal return information.

This is the “app composes, user sends” principle. It keeps RxOrderly out of the middle of every transaction with your healthcare providers — which is exactly where you don’t need a middleman.

4. Cloud Backups Will Be End-to-End Encrypted

When automatic cloud backups arrive in a future version (Chapter 21), they will be encrypted on your phone before being uploaded, using a key only you hold. Rivka SVCS, the company that makes the app, will not be able to decrypt your backup — not even with a court order, because we won’t have the key.

This is the same approach used by Signal, Apple iCloud Advanced Data Protection, and a handful of other privacy-first services. It’s the strongest practical guarantee available.

5. Same Architecture for Everyone

RxOrderly may, in the future, offer white-label versions of itself to pharmacies and clinics — an RxOrderly with their branding. Those white-label versions use exactly the same privacy architecture as this one. There is no “pharmacy-back-end” mode where the pharmacy gets to see your data. The promise is the same in all editions.

6. AI Conforms to These Rules, Not the Other Way Around

If RxOrderly ever uses artificial intelligence to help with anything — reading a medication label from a photo, summarizing a doctor’s instructions — the work happens on your phone first. Anything that does need to leave your phone is stripped of personal details (names, dates, identifiers) before it leaves. AI is a tool in the same toolbox as the rest of the app; it conforms to the privacy rules, never the other way around.

The Bottom Line

You can use RxOrderly as if it were a paper notebook you keep in your pocket: yours, private, only ever seen by you and the people you choose to show it to. That’s not a marketing tagline; it’s how the app is built.

Chapter 23

Tips from Jacques

If you were standing next to me at the coffee shop and had just installed RxOrderly, here’s what I’d tell you. Some of these you’ve seen in the chapters above; they’re worth gathering in one place.

Set It Up Completely

I know I keep coming back to this one. It really is the whole game. An app that knows three of your medications and one of your doctors is fine; an app that knows everything is a partner that does work for you every day. The setup is honestly just an hour or so, spread out however you like.

Use One Routine Per Time, Not One Per Pill

If you take four medications with breakfast, make one routine called “Morning — with breakfast” with all four medications attached. Not four separate reminders fighting for your attention. Far gentler on the nerves.

Match Routine Times to Things You Already Do

“Breakfast,” “with the morning paper,” “after my walk,” “before bed” — these stick. Arbitrary clock times don’t. Tie a routine to a thing you already do every day and you’ll never miss it.

Print the Medical ID, Replace It Every Six Months

Print on slightly heavier paper, trim with scissors, slip it in your wallet behind your driver’s license. Every six months, when the date on the card is more than half a year old, print a fresh one. If you ever need it, the person rifling your wallet will find it in fifteen seconds.

Record Costs as You Go

When you pick up a refill, take the extra ten seconds to log the cost too. By year-end, your Find Savings page will be a real, accurate picture of what you spent — useful for taxes, useful for switching plans, useful for arguing with insurance.

Use Mental Notes the Moment a Thought Arises

The whole point of Mental Notes is to capture thoughts when they appear, even if “the moment” is two in the morning or in the middle of a TV show. Five seconds to type a sentence, save, done. By your next visit, you’ll have a tidy list rather than the usual scramble of “I know there was something I wanted to mention.”

Update Pharmacies and Prescribers When They Change

If you switch pharmacies, take two minutes to add the new one and switch each affected medication over. If a doctor retires or you change practices, edit the prescriber. RxOrderly only does the right thing if it knows the right things.

Deactivate, Don’t Delete

When you stop a medication, switch Active off rather than deleting. Your history stays intact — useful for the doctor, useful for you. Same for prescribers and pharmacies.

Back Up After Big Changes

After you finish the initial setup, create a backup. After each major doctor visit where things changed, create another. With a password you can remember. Save the file somewhere you can find it in a year — email it to yourself, save it in your Dropbox, hand a copy to a trusted family member. Phones get lost. The backup is the safety net.

Schedule Doctor Visit Prep the Day Before

The night before an appointment, take five minutes with the Doctor Visit Prep wizard (Chapter 15). Print the sheet, fold it, slip it in your bag. Your doctor will thank you and you’ll cover everything you meant to cover.

Don’t Be Afraid to Email Me

I answer every email at support@rxorderly.com myself. There is no support team yet — just me, with a cup of coffee, working through whatever’s in the inbox. If something is broken, if something is confusing, if there’s a feature you wish existed — tell me. I’d rather hear from you than not.
